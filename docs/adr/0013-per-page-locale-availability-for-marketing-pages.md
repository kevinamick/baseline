# Per-page locale availability for marketing pages (amends ADR-0011)

The new SEO/marketing surface — competitor comparison pages (`/compare/{braintrust,langsmith,humanloop,langfuse}`), category landers (`/llm-evaluation`, `/llm-as-judge`, `/prompt-optimization`, `/rubric-based-evaluation`), and problem-led non-technical pages (`/reduce-ai-hallucinations`, `/ai-agent-testing`) — ships **English-only at launch**, with a planned fast-follow that localizes it into the existing `es` and `fr`. To make that fast-follow a per-page content flip rather than a re-architecture, each marketing page declares the **set of locales it actually exists in** (`["en"]` at launch); its metadata and `generateStaticParams`/`dynamicParams` derive `hreflang`, self-canonical, and prerendering from that set, so a page emits alternates **only for locales where its translation exists**. This deliberately departs from ADR-0011's invariant that *every path exists in every locale* with a full `hreflang` cluster (the all-locales `buildAlternates`, which remains the default for the tri-lingual product funnel — home, pricing, privacy).

**Why partial `hreflang` instead of all-locales-now or no-`hreflang`-ever.** The commercial-intent searches these pages target ("Baseline vs Braintrust", "LLM evaluation platform") are overwhelmingly English, so translating ten pages into `es`/`fr` up front is low-ROI work that delays launch; English-only now captures nearly all the available traffic. But "English-only" must not mean "emit `es`/`fr` alternates that 404 or serve English" — a broken alternate cluster gets soft-404'd and can cause Google to **suppress the whole `hreflang` group, hurting even the `en` page**. The locale-set primitive resolves the tension: at launch each page advertises only `en` (self-canonical, no `es`/`fr` alternates); as a translation lands, that page flips to `["en","es","fr"]` independently, and there is never a window where `hreflang` points at a page that does not yet exist.

**Consequence — the failure mode this exists to prevent.** A future reader will see marketing pages pinned to `["en"]` mid-migration and may assume it's an oversight, "helpfully" enabling all locales to match the rest of the app — recreating exactly the broken-`hreflang` state the mechanism prevents. The locale set is the single source of truth for a marketing page's `hreflang`, canonical, and prerendered params: widen it **only** when the corresponding `es`/`fr` content is actually present. The product funnel is unaffected and keeps using the all-locales `buildAlternates`.
