# Two billing meters: Eval Points for platform work, metered dollars for managed tokens

Usage billing (pricing model in issue #137) runs on two independent meters per Team, plus one invariant that binds them.

**Meter 1 — Eval Points.** Eval Runs consume Eval Points (orchestration + criterion scoring). Optimization Runs do **not** consume Eval Points; they draw from their own per-plan run allowance, with a per-plan `budget_rollouts` ceiling bounding the orchestration cost of a single flat-priced run. We rejected metering Rollouts as Eval Points (cost-accurate but makes the published "included runs" line dishonest) and hybrid double-debiting (every run hits two meters; unexplainable on an invoice).

> **Superseded for Optimization Runs by ADR-0016.** The included run-count allowance stays as the included benefit, but a run *past* it now meters Eval Points (`scored_rollouts × (base + per_criterion×|criteria|)`) instead of a flat dollars-per-run overage. The flat `optimizationRunOverageUsd` rate is removed; optimization overage is points. Within-allowance runs remain point-free. (Free stays hard-walled — the points-overage path is paid-only.)

**Meter 2 — managed token spend, in dollars.** Token costs from Managed Keys are metered as actual provider cost and billed as their own invoice line at cost × (1 + plan markup). They are **not** converted into Eval Points: embedding token costs in a prepaid point allotment means solvency depends on formula constants staying ahead of provider prices — a Team burning its *included* points on token-heavy work could cost more in provider spend than its subscription, with no overage ever triggered. A separate metered line is margin-positive by arithmetic, immune to model repricing.

**Invariant: flat fees never absorb token costs.** Tokens are either the customer's (BYO Key) or metered and passed through at cost + markup (Managed Key). No third state. Any future pricing change is checked against this. The published formula's `Points_tokens` term is dropped accordingly: an Eval Point is a pure measure of platform work (`points = Σ rows × (base + per-criterion)`), computable *before* a run starts and identical across key modes — token economics live entirely on the dollar meter.

**Overage is opt-in and cap-denominated.** All plans hard-stop when included allotments are exhausted — paid plans don't silently bill overage. Opting in *is* setting an Overage Cap ("allow up to $N/month"); unbounded overage is never a toggle's implicit meaning, only an explicitly typed large cap. This gives one enforcement model across every meter — the ledger's reserve step (ADR-0009) fails past a known ceiling — and one customer guarantee: the worst-case invoice is the subscription plus ceilings the Team set themselves. Every limit event (a run refused or stopped on quota, points or runs or managed spend) emails the Team's Contributors, so a quota-blocked Schedule announces itself rather than silently flatlining a monitoring dashboard.

*Resolved in S6 (#183):* the platform meters share **one** Overage Cap — a single dollar number covers Eval Point and Optimization Run overage combined, priced at the plan's per-unit rates; a negative ledger balance *is* the overage, and the reserve checks the projected dollar total across both meters under ordered per-org locks. Two caps for two platform meters would make the Team predict a split of work they cannot foresee; one number is the promise they actually want to make. The **Managed Spend Cap stays separate** (Meter 2 is pass-through dollars with its own trust escalation, S8/S11) — "ceilings the Team set themselves" means exactly two numbers: the Overage Cap and the Managed Spend Cap. Settled overage (never mere reservations) projects into invoice lines and pushes to Stripe as one invoice item per period and meter, the unit rate snapshotted at first push so later plan changes never reprice an old period's overage.

**Managed-token guardrails.** Billing the markup line in arrears creates credit risk (burn tokens, decline the card) and shock-bill risk; both are bounded at the dollar meter, not the points meter:

1. **Managed Spend Cap** — a per-Team hard monthly ceiling on managed token spend, defaulted per plan, customer-visible and raisable up to a trust ceiling. Runs refuse to start (or stop between units) once the cap is reached.
2. **Threshold billing** — accrued managed usage is invoiced immediately when it crosses a fixed threshold rather than waiting for month-end, capping maximum credit exposure at the threshold; a declined card halts further managed runs.

   *Refined in #215:* a failing payment method (`managed_payment_failed_at` set, or the subscription `past_due`/`unpaid`) also halts the **other** unpaid-credit meter — **overage** (Eval Point / Optimization Run usage past the included allotment) — across **both** key modes, BYO included. Overage is arrears-billed platform credit, so a Team on a bad card would otherwise keep accruing it (a BYO run's only cost to us is the orchestration the overage meter bills for). **Prepaid/included** usage is untouched: the reserve simply suppresses the plan's overage unit rates while payment is failing, so it hard-stops at the included balance exactly as an uncapped Team does, and recovery (invoice paid / status active) reopens overage automatically with no manual step. The principle: billing blocks the extension of **unpaid credit**, never **prepaid** activity.
3. **Gateway accrual** — every managed call already passes through our worker, so cost accrues per call, is checked against the cap before and during runs, and a pre-run estimate is shown alongside the exact point cost.
4. **Trust escalation** — caps start low and rise with invoice history; the subscription itself is charged upfront at signup, proving the card before the first managed token burns.

**v1 ships both key modes.** Managed Keys give paid plans low-friction onboarding (so per-token metering is v1-critical); BYO Keys exist at launch so the Free plan can ship as BYO-only per #137. Free Teams never run on Managed Keys — no card on file would mean unbounded token exposure — so the Free tier's hard point cap only ever bounds orchestration cost, which is negligible. We accepted the larger v1 (metering *and* per-Team key storage) over deferring either the Free tier or the managed onboarding path.

**Retention: soft-delete at the window, purge 30 days later.** Runs older than the Plan's Retention Window (14 days / 90 days / 3 years) are marked soft-deleted (a `deleted_at`-style column) and disappear from the product; a scheduled job permanently purges rows 30 days after soft-deletion. The UI labels the boundary ("showing the last 90 days — your plan's retention window"). Downgrade cliffs get the loud treatment: a downgrade that moves a large swath of history out of window (Scale's 3 years → Builder's 90 days) bulk-soft-deletes it and emails Contributors with the count and the purge date; re-upgrading within the 30-day grace clears the soft-delete flags for anything back inside the window. Invariant, final form: **billing state changes never destroy data instantly** — permanent deletion happens only by the scheduled retention job, always ≥30 days after the data left visibility, never as a synchronous side effect of a plan change or payment failure. The purge job doubles as the GDPR data-minimization mechanism.

**Plan changes: upgrades immediate, downgrades at period end.** An upgrade applies instantly — Stripe prorates the charge, and the ledger receives a delta grant (`new included − old included`) into the current period. Downgrades and cancellations (cancellation = downgrade to Free) are Stripe-scheduled for period end; the paid-for grant is never clawed back mid-period. Downgrades are gated on the target plan's constraints at schedule time (e.g. Free's 1-seat cap shows an explicit "remove members to continue" wall) and re-checked at execution; a violation at execution lands the Team in the standard fail-closed state (runs blocked, Contributors emailed) until resolved. Invariant: **billing never destroys Team data or membership — it only blocks activity.** Humans remove members; billing just refuses to proceed.

**Period mechanics: no rollover, plan-start anchor.** Included allotments are use-it-or-lose-it — rollover would demand expiry tranches in the ledger and murky forecasting; the generous version of a plan is a bigger number, not a points savings account. Every Team's cycle anchors to when its plan started: paid Teams use the Stripe billing period from the mirror, Free Teams use their `organizations.created_at` anniversary (day-clamped, Stripe-style — created Jan 31 resets Feb 28). Calendar-month anchoring was rejected as gameable: a Free signup on the last day of the month would collect two full grants in two days. Reservations settle against their origin period even when a run finishes after the boundary; in-flight work never touches the new period's grant.

**Source of truth, three-way split.** Plan *definitions* (included allotments, seat limits, retention, markup, default caps per tier) are code constants — one const list with derived types, mapped to Stripe price IDs via env — so pricing knobs change by reviewed PR, not migration or dashboard edit. Subscription *state* is owned by Stripe and mirrored into Postgres by webhook (`checkout.session.completed`, `customer.subscription.updated`, `customer.subscription.deleted`, `invoice.payment_failed`); quota checks read only the mirror, never the Stripe API. Usage *state* (Point Ledger, caps, accrued managed spend) lives in Postgres exclusively and is pushed to Stripe as invoice line items, never read back for enforcement — the atomic reserve step (ADR-0009) must be a single local transaction. Enforcement fails closed: a Team with a missing or unpaid mirror row gets blocked runs plus the notification email, never a free pass.

**Provider-agnostic by construction.** No LLM provider is structural in the billing model. Keys are (Team, provider) pairs in the vault (provider from a single-source const list); gateway accrual prices each call from a versioned per-provider/per-model price table, snapshotting the unit prices used into the accrual row so provider repricing never rewrites history; Managed Keys are per-provider platform keys; invoices itemize token spend by provider and model. v1 may ship with one provider in the list, but nothing — schema, metering, invoicing — needs touching to add a second.

## Decision principle: billing transparency

Chosen explicitly as a binding criterion for future billing decisions: customer trust through auditable billing. Every charge maps to a line a customer can verify (a point ledger they can inspect, a token bill reconcilable against provider list prices and the published markup). When a billing design choice trades margin opacity against customer legibility, legibility wins.
